# Lexicon Wave 2 Politics, Government, and Law Literature Review

## Phase 1 — Claims and inertia

1. State, government, polity, administration, and public authority require
   different institutional, territorial, membership, office, and authority
   questions. Skeptical pressure: state and government are often used loosely.
2. Citizenship, nationality, residency, membership, and political status
   overlap but have different authorities, incidents, territories, and times.
   Skeptical pressure: jurisdictions relate nationality and citizenship
   differently.
3. Representation, constituency, mandate, delegation, and election require
   separate represented party, authorization, activity, audience, omission,
   and selection questions. Skeptical pressure: delegate, trustee, descriptive,
   symbolic, and claim-making accounts compete.
4. Power, authority, legitimacy, influence, and coercion cannot be reduced to
   effectiveness or popularity. Skeptical pressure: descriptive and normative
   legitimacy and multiple concepts of authority remain live.
5. Law, precedent, judgment, remedy, enforcement, policy, program, service,
   regulation, and implementation require source, jurisdiction, institution,
   procedure, instrument, delivery, and effect distinctions. Skeptical
   pressure: theories disagree about law's relation to morality and coercion.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Continue routing these questions through Organization,
Policy/Rule, Hohfeldian incidents, Justice, and Power without a political or
legal institutional owner.
