# Lexicon Wave 4 Health, Disability, Treatment, and Care Literature Review

## Phase 1 — Claims and inertia

1. Health, disease, disorder, condition, and well-being require distinct
   state, classification, mechanism, experience, and evaluative-account questions.
2. Functioning, impairment, activity limitation, disability, and participation
   are contextual and cannot be inferred from diagnosis alone.
3. Prevention, intervention, treatment, therapy, and rehabilitation differ by
   target, timing, mechanism, purpose, authority, and intended outcome.
4. Care, service, provider, recipient, pathway, and continuity distinguish a
   relationship and delivery organization from an individual intervention.
5. Clinical outcome, response, recovery, remission, and quality of life require
   declared measures, baselines, timing, attribution, and affected perspective.

Skeptical pressure: health and disability models compete; diagnostic
classifications are versioned; intervention categories overlap; response,
recovery, remission, and quality of life are condition- and instrument-specific.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Continue routing these terms through Disease Mechanism,
Clinical Diagnosis, Welfare, Generic Service, and Generic Recovery, leaving
readers unable to distinguish health state, functioning, intervention, care
delivery, and outcome.

## Phase 4 — Evidence position

Claims with strong support: 5/5. Contrary evidence requires contextual
disability, versioned classification, distinct endpoint/assessment/outcome,
and person-reported quality-of-life perspectives.

HIGH RISK claims: 5. Risk is controlled by candidate maturity, source/version
qualification, explicit no-diagnosis/no-treatment/no-individual-risk language,
and routing all live decisions to qualified authorities.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Continue using diagnostic, mechanism, welfare, service, and
recovery owners as substitutes for five different health-domain questions.

INERTIA RISK: readers infer functioning from diagnosis, conflate treatment with
rehabilitation, care with an intervention, and response with recovery or quality of life.

RECOMMENDATION: PROCEED with five primary Tables; hold Screening/Test because
the existing clinical-diagnosis owner covers its current reader job and a new
testing pathway would require separate diagnostic-accuracy evidence.
