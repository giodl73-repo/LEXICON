# Lexicon Wave 3 Formal Reasoning and Mathematics Literature Review

## Phase 1 — Claims and inertia

1. Statement, proposition, premise, conclusion, and argument require distinct
   expression, content, position, and inferential-organization questions.
2. Deduction, induction, abduction, and analogy differ in consequence,
   ampliation, explanatory appeal, sampling/generalization, and mapping.
3. Validity, soundness, proof, demonstration, and refutation require a named
   formal system, semantics, assumptions, and proof standard.
4. Consistency, contradiction, satisfiability, and completeness differ between
   syntactic, semantic, theory-relative, and task-relative readings.
5. Number/numeral/quantity/magnitude/measurement and set/collection/membership/
   relation/function/structure require level distinctions without taking over
   existing metrology, collection-identity, or mathematical-solving owners.

Skeptical pressure: nonclassical logics alter consequence; inductive and
abductive warrant is contested; soundness/completeness are system-relative;
mathematical structuralism and set-theoretic foundations offer competing
accounts; “measurement” has discipline-specific operational owners.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Keep routing formal questions through Claim/Evidence,
Learning/Reasoning, Collection, Quantity Value, Mathematical Relation, and
Model even when the reader needs formal role, consequence, proof-status, or
mathematical-object distinctions.
