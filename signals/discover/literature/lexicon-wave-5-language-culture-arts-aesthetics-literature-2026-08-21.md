# Lexicon Wave 5 Language, Culture, Arts, and Aesthetics Literature Review

## Phase 1 — Claims and inertia

1. Phone, phoneme, grapheme, orthography, and pronunciation require separate
   observed-sound, contrastive-system, writing-unit, writing-convention, and
   realized-form questions.
2. Lexeme, word, phrase, clause, sentence, and utterance occupy different
   lexical, grammatical, textual, and communicative levels; boundaries are
   language- and task-sensitive.
3. Syntax, grammar, acceptability, grammaticality, and usage separate a
   structural subsystem, a broader conventional system, evidence from
   judgments, conformity under an analysis, and attested practice.
4. Translation, paraphrase, equivalence, adaptation, and transcreation differ
   in language change, transformation scope, relation claimed, purpose, and
   responsibility; none guarantees sameness of effect or meaning.
5. Culture, subculture, heritage, tradition, and cultural practice require
   community, transmission, recognition, change, and rights context rather
   than treatment as fixed natural kinds.
6. Ritual, ceremony, observance, sacred practice, and symbolic action overlap
   but differ in conventional sequence, occasion, participation, authority,
   sacred orientation, and interpretive function.
7. Artwork, medium, form, composition, style, and genre separate an identified
   work from its material/technical vehicle, organization, manner, and
   classificatory tradition.
8. Aesthetic property, experience, judgment, taste, and value are competing
   explanatory centers, not interchangeable labels or simple measurements.
9. Creation, authorship, performance, interpretation, and reception separate
   production, responsibility/attribution, realization, sense-making, and
   audience encounter.

Skeptical pressure: linguistic units are theory- and language-sensitive;
Unicode boundaries are implementation defaults, not linguistic universals;
cultural categories cannot be imposed independently of communities; art and
aesthetic theory contain persistent competing accounts; legal authorship does
not exhaust creative contribution.

INERTIA SCENARIO: Continue routing these questions through morphology,
discourse, meaning/reference, register/genre, abstract work, material artifact,
and contribution/legacy owners, forcing one owner to answer several different
levels and perspectives.
