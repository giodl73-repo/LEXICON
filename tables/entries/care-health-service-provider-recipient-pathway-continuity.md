# Care, Health Service, Provider, Recipient, Pathway, and Continuity

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Care is an attentive, relational, and practical response to health, functioning, support, or comfort needs. A health service is an organized provision intended to promote, assess, maintain, restore, or support health and functioning. A provider is a person, team, organization, or system authorized or recognized to deliver a service. A recipient is the person, family, group, or population receiving or intended to receive it. A care pathway organizes expected steps and transitions for a defined need or population. Continuity is coherent connection of care, information, and relationships over time and settings.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `health-care` | Which needs are attended to through what relationship, practical support, and responsibility? | attentive health-support relationship/practice |
| `health-service` | Which organized provision delivers what health-related function to whom under what access conditions? | organized health provision |
| `care-provider` | Which person, team, organization, or system delivers which service under what competence and authority? | service-delivering party |
| `care-recipient` | Which person, family, group, or population receives or is intended to receive which care or service? | care-receiving party |
| `care-pathway` | Which expected sequence and transitions organize care for which need or population under what pathway authority? | organized care route |
| `care-continuity` | How are relationships, management, and information connected across encounters, providers, settings, and time? | longitudinal care connection |

## Root factorization

```text
care-delivery-use
  := recipient or population, need, goals, setting, and time
   x care relationship, attentiveness, responsibility, support, comfort, and preferences
   x service function, scope, access, eligibility, capacity, quality, and funding context
   x provider identity, role, competence, authority, team, and accountability
   x recipient agency, consent, communication, supporters, burden, and experience
   x pathway entry, steps, transitions, referral, coordination, deviation, and exit
   x relational, informational, and management continuity, handoff, and longitudinal review
   x evidence, equity, safety, uncertainty, provenance, and no-advice boundary
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Care vs. treatment | treatment may be care | broader relational/practical response vs. condition-targeted intervention |
| Care vs. service | services deliver care functions | relationship/practice vs. organized provision |
| Provider vs. practitioner | practitioners may provide | delivery role for a service vs. professional identity |
| Recipient vs. patient | patients are recipients in clinical contexts | broad receiving party vs. clinical relationship/status |
| Pathway vs. treatment plan | plans may follow pathways | population/need route vs. individualized intended course |
| Continuity vs. continuum | both concern extended care | coherent connection over time vs. range/sequence of service types |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Generic service, organization roles, workflow, access/entitlement, consent, and intervention retain their owners. This Table stops before determining eligibility, assigning a provider, designing an individual care plan, making referrals, or judging quality/safety of actual care.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify recipient/population, need, goals, service context, authority, and time.
2. Select care relationship, service, provider/recipient role, pathway, or continuity question.
3. Record access, competence, consent, steps, transitions, information, and accountability separately.
4. Distinguish expected pathway from actual journey and continuity from mere repeated contact.
5. Preserve preferences, deviations, fragmentation, equity, uncertainty, and qualified decisions.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Primary-care framework | defines access, comprehensiveness, coordination, continuity | separates relationship, provision, and pathway roles |
| Service-delivery system | organizes providers, capacity, funding, and handoffs | keeps the recipient and care relationship visible |
| Individual record/plan | documents actual care and intended steps | remains authoritative for a person's course |

## Failure signs

- care and treatment are synonyms;
- provider means only an individual clinician;
- recipient agency and preferences disappear;
- pathway is treated as the person's actual course;
- repeated visits prove informational, relational, or management continuity;
- the Table assigns or evaluates real care.
