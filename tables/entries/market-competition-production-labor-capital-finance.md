# Market, Competition, Production, Labor, Capital, and Finance

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

A market is an institutionally bounded setting or mechanism for offers,
exchange, allocation, and price formation. Competition is rivalry or constrained
independent action among participants for opportunities or resources. Production
transforms inputs into outputs under an account. Labor is human work or service
under declared social and statistical scope. Capital is a stock or claim used
in production or expected return under a specified account. Finance organizes
funding, claims, instruments, intermediation, payment, and risk across time.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `market` | Which participants, goods or claims, rules, venue, and mechanism constitute this market? | exchange and allocation institution |
| `competition` | Which participants rival for which opportunity under which rules, constraints, and market boundary? | rivalry relation or process |
| `production` | Which inputs are transformed into which outputs by which activities under which production account? | input-output transformation process |
| `labor` | Which human work or service is supplied, organized, compensated, or recorded under which account? | human work contribution |
| `capital` | Which accumulated resource, productive asset, or financial claim is treated as capital under which account? | account-relative stock or claim |
| `finance` | How are funds, claims, instruments, payments, intermediation, and risks organized across parties and time? | funding and claims system |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Market vs. exchange | exchange occurs in markets | bounded institution/mechanism vs. particular reciprocal arrangement |
| Competition vs. comparison | either relates alternatives | rivalry for opportunity/resources vs. analytic relation |
| Production vs. work | work can contribute to production | account-defined input-output transformation vs. expended effort or service |
| Labor vs. task | tasks organize work | human work under social/statistical relation vs. bounded unit of planned action |
| Capital vs. wealth | either concerns valued stocks | productive resource or financial claim under an account vs. broader net holdings |
| Finance vs. value | finance uses valuations | organization of funding and claims vs. evaluative relation |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Cost, price, value, incentives, collective action, operational resources,
stock-flow accounting, organizations, transactions, and risk retain their
owners. This Table does not select an economic school, define a universal
market, classify industries or instruments, calculate value, or provide
financial, labor, competition, or policy advice.

## Selection procedure

1. Name account, boundary, participants, institutions, jurisdiction, and period.
2. Select market setting, rivalry, transformation, human work, capital stock/claim, or finance system.
3. Record units, flows, stocks, rules, information, power, and missing parties.
4. Separate observed exchange and price from the market account used to interpret them.
5. Preserve unpaid work, externalities, informal activity, distribution, and uncertainty.
6. Route valuation, risk, and policy decisions to their owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| National accounts | defines sectors, production boundaries, labor, and capital | makes account and period mandatory |
| Industrial organization | analyzes markets and competition | retains mechanism, boundary, and institutional variation |
| Financial statistics | defines institutions, instruments, assets, and liabilities | separates finance system from value or return |

## Failure signs

- every exchange setting is assumed to be one competitive market;
- competition is inferred from the number of participants alone;
- production ignores its account boundary or residuals;
- labor is reduced to paid employment;
- capital, money, wealth, and finance share one field;
- an economic description becomes investment or policy advice.
