# Responsibility, Answerability, Blame, Liability, and Excuse

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Moral responsibility attributes conduct or an outcome to an agent in a way
that makes normative appraisal appropriate under an account. Answerability is
the standing or demand to give reasons or an account. Blame is a family of
negative responses to perceived fault or wrongdoing. Normative liability is
exposure to a response, burden, sanction, or changed normative position under
a framework. An excuse accepts or leaves the relevant conduct while reducing
or defeating attribution, fault, or the appropriateness of a response.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `moral-responsibility` | Which agent is attributable for which conduct or outcome under what capacity, control, awareness, and normative account? | morally appraisable attribution |
| `answerability` | Who must answer to whom for what by giving which reasons, explanation, justification, or account? | reason-giving standing or demand |
| `blame` | Which negative appraisal or response is directed at whom for what fault or wrongdoing, and when is it appropriate? | fault-directed normative response |
| `normative-liability` | Which subject is exposed to which burden, sanction, remedy, or changed normative position under which framework and conditions? | exposure to normative consequence |
| `excuse` | Which condition reduces or defeats which attribution, fault, blame, or liability without necessarily denying the conduct? | responsibility-modifying consideration |

## Root factorization

```text
responsibility-response-use
  := agent or subject, conduct or outcome, affected parties, and context
   x attribution, causal role, capacity, control, awareness, intention, and alternatives
   x normative account, duty or expectation, fault, appraisal, and reasons
   x answerer, forum, standing, demand, account, challenge, and response
   x blame form, target, basis, expression, consequence, and proportionality
   x liability source, burden or sanction, conditions, procedure, and authority
   x excuse condition, relevance, scope, evidence, and residual responsibility
   x luck, coercion, incapacity, ignorance, collective action, uncertainty, and provenance
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Causal responsibility vs. moral responsibility | either can connect agent and outcome | causal contribution vs. normatively appraisable attribution |
| Responsibility vs. accountability | either concerns being held to account | appraisal-bearing attribution vs. governed reporting/oversight relationship |
| Answerability vs. blame | blame may demand an answer | reason-giving standing vs. negative fault-directed response |
| Blame vs. liability | blame can accompany sanction | appraisal/response vs. exposure under a normative or institutional framework |
| Excuse vs. justification | either can answer criticism | reduces attribution/fault vs. claims the conduct was permissible or right |
| Excuse vs. exemption | either can defeat blame | occasion-specific modifying condition vs. status outside an accountability practice |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Causation, organizational accountability, Hohfeldian liability, deontic
obligation, human error, legal judgment, and clinical capacity determinations
retain their owners. This Table stops before assigning guilt, punishment,
damages, professional discipline, legal liability, or a diagnosis affecting
responsibility.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify agent, conduct or outcome, affected parties, context, and normative framework.
2. Separate causal contribution from moral responsibility and governed accountability.
3. Record capacity, control, awareness, intention, alternatives, coercion, and relevant luck.
4. Select attribution, answerability, blame response, liability exposure, or excuse question.
5. Distinguish excuse, justification, exemption, mitigation, denial, and forgiveness.
6. Preserve procedure, authority, evidence, proportionality, collective/member distinctions, and uncertainty.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Moral responsibility theory | analyzes agency, control, quality of will, and holding responses | separates attribution, answerability, blame, and excuse |
| Organizational governance | assigns responsibility and accountability to roles | keeps moral appraisal distinct from reporting and authority contracts |
| Legal system | defines liabilities, defenses, procedures, and sanctions | requires jurisdiction, authority, version, and qualified determination |

## Failure signs

- causal involvement automatically entails moral blame;
- organizational responsibility and moral responsibility share one test;
- a demand for explanation is treated as proof of fault;
- blame, sanction, liability, and punishment are interchangeable;
- an excuse denies that the conduct occurred or automatically justifies it;
- group responsibility is copied to every member;
- the Table adjudicates guilt, liability, discipline, or punishment.
