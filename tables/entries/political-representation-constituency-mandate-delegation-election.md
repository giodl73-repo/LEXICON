# Political Representation, Constituency, Mandate, Delegation, and Election

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Political representation makes persons, interests, claims, or perspectives present in political action through a representative relation. A constituency is the represented body defined by an electoral, territorial, organizational, or claim-making basis. A mandate is asserted authorization or direction for action. Delegation entrusts bounded decision authority or work to another. An election is a governed selection process using votes or another electoral rule.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `political-representation` | Who represents whom or what, before which audience, through what activity and standard? | political making-present relation |
| `constituency` | Which persons, territory, interests, or claims constitute the represented body under what rule? | represented political body |
| `political-mandate` | Which authorization or direction is claimed from which source for what action, scope, and term? | asserted governing authorization |
| `political-delegation` | Who entrusts which authority or task to whom under what limits and accountability? | bounded political entrustment |
| `election` | Which offices or options are selected by whom under which franchise, ballot, rule, and procedure? | governed electoral selection |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Representation vs. resemblance | descriptive representation may resemble | acting/making present vs. similarity alone |
| Constituency vs. electorate | voters may be constituents | represented body vs. eligible voting body |
| Mandate vs. preference | mandates may cite preferences | claimed authorization/direction vs. attitude |
| Delegation vs. representation | a representative may be delegated power | entrustment vs. making represented interests present |
| Election vs. representation | elections may authorize representatives | selection process vs. continuing relation/activity |
| Delegate vs. trustee | both represent | instructed responsiveness vs. independent judgment account |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Generic delegation, choice, voting measurement, organization, and communication retain their owners. This Table stops before determining electoral validity, apportionment legality, a representative's duty, election outcome, or preferred electoral design.

## Selection procedure

1. Name representative, represented party/content, audience, setting, and time.
2. Identify constituency rule and what voices, interests, or perspectives are omitted.
3. Select representation, constituency, mandate, delegation, or election question.
4. Record authorization, activity, responsiveness, accountability, procedure, and standard separately.
5. Preserve delegate, trustee, descriptive, symbolic, substantive, and claim-making accounts.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Representation theory | supplies formal, descriptive, symbolic, and substantive accounts | requires all parties, activity, audience, and omissions |
| Election system | defines franchise, ballot, aggregation, and certification | separates selection from representation quality |
| Principal-agent model | analyzes delegation and accountability | does not exhaust political representation |

## Failure signs

- winning an election proves adequate representation;
- constituency means only current voters;
- a vote total establishes a substantive mandate without interpretation;
- delegation and representation are interchangeable;
- omitted or constructed constituencies are invisible;
- the Table adjudicates an election or recommends a system.
