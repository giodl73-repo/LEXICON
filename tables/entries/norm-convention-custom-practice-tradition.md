# Norm, Convention, Custom, Practice, and Tradition

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

A norm is an expectation or standard with a basis for conformity, criticism,
or sanction. A convention is a socially sustained regularity among alternatives
whose persistence depends on mutual expectations, coordination, acceptance, or
history. A custom is an established pattern associated with a community or
setting. A practice is an organized way of doing something with participants,
activities, materials, and standards. A tradition is a practice, belief,
account, or form represented as transmitted across time.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `social-norm` | Which subjects are expected to do or avoid what, on what basis, with which expectations, criticism, or sanctions? | socially sustained standard |
| `convention` | Which regularity among alternatives is sustained by which mutual expectations, coordination, acceptance, or history? | expectation-dependent regularity |
| `custom` | Which established pattern is associated with which community, setting, occasion, and continuity? | established communal pattern |
| `social-practice` | Which participants, activities, materials, competencies, purposes, and standards constitute the organized way of doing? | organized recurrent activity |
| `tradition` | Which practice, belief, account, or form is represented as transmitted, received, interpreted, and changed across which generations? | historically transmitted formation |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Norm vs. regularity | norms may produce regular behavior | standard with expectations/criticism vs. observed recurrence alone |
| Norm vs. rule | either can guide conduct | socially sustained standard vs. explicit formulation under an authority or system |
| Norm vs. convention | conventions can be normative | conformity standard vs. expectation-dependent selection among alternatives |
| Custom vs. practice | customs are enacted in activity | established communal pattern vs. organized way of doing with competencies and materials |
| Practice vs. habit | either recurs | socially organized activity vs. individual disposition or repetition |
| Tradition vs. unchanged inheritance | traditions claim continuity | interpreted transmission with possible change vs. exact copying |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Institutional norms, policies, rules, deontic force, register/usage, ritual, and
future cultural owners retain their scopes. This Table stops before catalogs of
etiquette, religious law, legal custom, named traditions, and any claim that
frequency alone creates normative force or that all norms share one sanction
mechanism.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify subjects, setting, group, time, conduct, and observation basis.
2. Test whether the issue is a standard, coordination regularity, established custom, organized practice, or transmitted tradition.
3. Record expectations, reasons, sanctions, exceptions, and contestation without inferring them from frequency alone.
4. Separate explicit rules and authorities from informal social maintenance.
5. For practices, record participants, activities, competence, materials, and standards.
6. For traditions, preserve transmission claims, change, competing lineages, and present interpretation.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Norm theory | analyzes expectations, conformity, and sanctions | separates normative basis from behavior and formal rule |
| Convention theory | explains stable alternatives and mutual expectations | keeps coordination, acceptance, and historical accounts open |
| Practice or tradition study | describes situated activity and transmission | requires participants, materials, lineage, variation, and evidence |

## Failure signs

- common behavior is automatically called a norm;
- every norm is treated as a written or legally enforceable rule;
- a convention is assumed to require explicit agreement;
- custom, habit, practice, and tradition become synonyms;
- traditions are represented as unchanged or internally homogeneous;
- one community's norm is generalized to all subjects or settings.
