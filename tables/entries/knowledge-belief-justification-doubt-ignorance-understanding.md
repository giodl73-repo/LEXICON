# Knowledge, Belief, Justification, Doubt, Ignorance, and Understanding

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

This Table separates attitudes and epistemic standings that are often treated
as one confidence scale. Belief is acceptance or commitment toward content;
knowledge is a factive standing under a declared account; justification is the
support or warrant relevant to an attitude; doubt withholds or unsettles
commitment; ignorance marks an attributed lack; understanding concerns grasp
of relations, reasons, or organization rather than possession of isolated facts.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `belief` | Which subject accepts which content, to what degree and under what disposition to revise? | attributed attitude |
| `knowledge` | Under which epistemic account does which subject know which true content? | factive epistemic standing |
| `epistemic-justification` | Which reasons, evidence, process, or warrant supports an attitude under which account? | support standing |
| `doubt` | Which commitment is withheld, reduced, or reopened for which reason? | unsettled attitude |
| `ignorance` | Which relevant content or competence is absent or unavailable to whom under what attribution rule? | attributed lack |
| `understanding` | Which relations, dependencies, explanations, or organization can the subject grasp and use? | relational cognitive achievement |

## Root factorization

```text
epistemic-standing-use
  := subject, community, or attributed knower
   x proposition, question, object, or domain
   x truth condition and world state where applicable
   x attitude, commitment, confidence, and suspension
   x evidence, reasons, warrant, and belief-forming process
   x epistemic account, standards, alternatives, and defeaters
   x relational model, explanation, use, and transfer
   x attribution source, time, context, and revision
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge vs. belief | both attribute content to a subject | factive standing under an account vs. attitude that may be false |
| Justification vs. explanation | both supply reasons | support for an attitude vs. account of why or how something occurs |
| Doubt vs. ignorance | both limit assertion | unsettled commitment toward content vs. attributed absence of relevant content or competence |
| Understanding vs. knowledge | either can support successful use | grasp of relations or organization vs. factive standing toward content |
| Confidence vs. justification | either affects acceptance | graded assessment vs. reasons or warrant under an epistemic account |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Truth, evidence, confidence, provenance, inference, and explanation retain their
existing owners. The Table does not select one theory of knowledge, define a
universal rational agent, diagnose cognition, or enumerate schools of
epistemology. Ignorance is not inferred merely from missing records.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify subject, exact content or domain, time, and attribution source.
2. Select attitude, factive standing, support, suspension, lack, or relational grasp.
3. Name the epistemic account and standards when asserting knowledge or justification.
4. Record evidence, process, alternatives, defeaters, and revision conditions.
5. Separate confidence from truth and support.
6. Preserve disagreement, uncertainty, and unobserved states.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary or thesaurus | relates knowledge, belief, doubt, and ignorance | separates subject, content, account, and standing |
| Epistemology | supplies competing analyses and counterexamples | preserves plurality rather than selecting one theory |
| Knowledge representation | records propositions and confidence | keeps represented content separate from a person's epistemic standing |

## Failure signs

- every confident belief is labeled knowledge;
- evidence is treated as automatic justification;
- missing data is reported as ignorance of a person;
- understanding is reduced to recall;
- one epistemic theory is presented as universal;
- doubt is treated as proof that content is false.
