# Validity, Soundness, Proof, Demonstration, and Refutation

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Validity is consequence preservation under a stated formal semantics or logic. Soundness of an argument combines validity with true premises under an account; soundness of a calculus says derivability preserves semantic validity. A proof is a checkable derivation under declared axioms and rules. A demonstration establishes or exhibits a result under a domain's accepted method. A refutation defeats a claim, argument, derivation, or universal statement through a valid counter-result under a stated standard.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `formal-validity` | Does the conclusion follow from the premises under which language, logic, semantics, and consequence relation? | formal consequence status |
| `soundness` | Which argument or calculus is sound in which sense, relative to which truth/semantic account? | truth-and-validity or calculus-preservation status |
| `proof` | Which finite or otherwise admitted derivation establishes which target from which axioms under which rules? | formal derivation object/process |
| `demonstration` | Which accepted method establishes or exhibits which result for which audience and domain? | method-governed establishment |
| `refutation` | Which counterexample, contradiction, derivation, or result defeats which target under what standard? | target-defeating result |

## Root factorization

```text
formal-establishment-use
  := target claim or formula, premises or assumptions, system, and context
   x formal language, syntax, semantics, interpretations, and consequence relation
   x axioms, inference rules, derivation steps, proof object, and checker
   x validity scope, premise truth account, argument-soundness sense, and calculus-soundness sense
   x demonstration method, admissible evidence, audience, reproducibility, and result
   x refutation target, counterexample or derivation, scope, and residual claims
   x constructive or nonconstructive status, automation, trust base, and verification
   x version, uncertainty, provenance, and limitations
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Validity vs. truth | valid inference preserves truth | relation/status of consequence vs. property of content under interpretation |
| Validity vs. soundness | sound arguments are valid | consequence status vs. validity plus premise truth account |
| Argument soundness vs. calculus soundness | both connect syntax and truth | property of argument vs. metatheorem about all derivations |
| Proof vs. evidence | proof can be evidence | derivation under rules vs. broader support artifact/status |
| Proof vs. demonstration | either establishes | formal derivation vs. domain-accepted exhibition/method |
| Refutation vs. failed proof | either lacks establishment | positive defeat under a standard vs. absence/failure of derivation |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Argument, inference, evidence, verification/validation, tests, and empirical falsification retain their owners. This Table stops before checking a submitted proof, deciding premise truth, choosing a logic, or declaring a real theory refuted.

## Selection procedure

1. State target, system, language, axioms, rules, semantics, and version.
2. Select validity, argument/calculus soundness, proof, demonstration, or refutation.
3. Record assumptions, derivation steps/object, checker/trust base, and scope.
4. Separate failed proof search from unprovability or refutation.
5. Preserve logic-relative status, residual claims, limitations, and provenance.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Model theory | defines semantic consequence and validity | keeps interpretations and language explicit |
| Proof theory | defines derivations and metatheoretic properties | separates proof object from soundness theorem |
| Domain practice | accepts demonstrations under disciplinary methods | does not silently promote them to formal proofs |

## Failure signs

- true conclusion makes an argument valid;
- valid argument is called sound without premise assessment;
- argument soundness and calculus soundness share one test;
- failed automated search proves unprovability;
- one counterexample refutes more than its quantified target;
- the Table certifies a submitted proof or theory.
