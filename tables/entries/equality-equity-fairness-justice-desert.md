# Equality, Equity, Fairness, Justice, and Desert

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Equality compares subjects as the same in a stated respect or requires equal
treatment, status, opportunity, or holdings under an account. Equity adjusts
attention, resources, process, or remedy in response to relevant differences
or disadvantage under a declared framework. Fairness evaluates terms,
procedures, interactions, or outcomes as appropriately impartial or balanced.
Justice evaluates what is due through distributive, corrective, procedural,
relational, or institutional principles. Desert links treatment or outcome to
a stated desert basis attributable to a subject.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `normative-equality` | Which subjects should be equal in which respect, domain, treatment, status, opportunity, or holdings under which account? | equality requirement |
| `equity` | Which relevant differences or disadvantages justify which adjusted attention, resources, process, or remedy under which framework? | difference-responsive adjustment principle |
| `fairness` | Which terms, procedures, interactions, or outcomes are appropriately impartial, reciprocal, consistent, or balanced by which criterion? | fair-treatment evaluation |
| `justice` | What is due to whom under which distributive, corrective, procedural, relational, or institutional principles? | justice evaluation |
| `desert` | Which subject deserves which treatment or outcome on what basis, with what attribution, proportionality, and scope? | desert-based claim |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Equality vs. identity | equal items may differ | sameness in a respect/treatment vs. being the same entity |
| Equality vs. equity | either can guide allocation | equal status or distribution vs. adjustment for relevant difference/disadvantage |
| Fairness vs. justice | either evaluates treatment | bounded impartiality/terms/procedure vs. broader account of what is due |
| Justice vs. legality | law may instantiate justice claims | normative due under principles vs. status under governing law |
| Desert vs. entitlement | either supports a claim | merit- or conduct-linked basis vs. institutionally recognized right or claim |
| Desert vs. need | either can distribute resources | attributable basis vs. condition requiring support |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Evaluation, allocation, Hohfeldian rights, deontic force, policy, welfare, and
future legal/political owners retain their scopes. This Table stops before
selecting a universal theory of justice, deciding a live allocation dispute,
determining unlawful discrimination, assigning remedy, or providing legal,
employment, educational, or public-policy advice.

## Selection procedure

1. State subjects, domain, goods or burdens, comparison class, boundary, and time.
2. Identify whether the question concerns equality, difference-responsive equity, fairness, justice, or desert.
3. Name the currency, criterion, principle, authority, and relevant differences.
4. Separate procedure, terms, relationship, distribution, correction, and outcome.
5. Test need, contribution, responsibility, opportunity, sufficiency, priority, and rights as distinct possible bases.
6. Preserve competing accounts, affected perspectives, uncertainty, and unresolved claims.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Equality theory | distinguishes equal treatment, status, opportunity, resources, and welfare | requires respect, currency, domain, and comparison class |
| Justice theory | supplies distributive, corrective, procedural, and relational principles | keeps form, institution, claim, and remedy question explicit |
| Allocation procedure | applies criteria to scarce goods or burdens | separates operational assignment from normative justification |

## Failure signs

- equality is asserted without saying equality of what and among whom;
- equity means any unequal treatment or any favorable outcome;
- fairness is used as an unexplained approval word;
- legality, justice, and legitimacy collapse into one status;
- desert is inferred from outcome, rank, or social status alone;
- the Table resolves a live legal, employment, education, or policy dispute.
