# Good, Benefit, Welfare, Harm, and Flourishing

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Good is favorable value under a stated evaluative account. A benefit is a
change, condition, or contribution that improves something for a subject or
purpose. Welfare concerns how well a subject is faring under a declared
account. Prudential harm makes that subject worse off under the same kind of
account. Flourishing is a richer account of doing or living well through
activities, relationships, capacities, achievements, or other goods over time.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `evaluative-good` | What is good, in which respect, for whom or independently of whom, under which evaluative account? | positively evaluated object or state |
| `benefit` | Which change, condition, or contribution improves what for which subject or purpose relative to what baseline? | subject- or purpose-relative improvement |
| `welfare` | How well is which subject faring over which scope and time under which account and evidence? | subject-relative prudential state |
| `prudential-harm` | Which event, condition, or deprivation makes which subject worse off relative to which baseline and welfare account? | welfare-relative worsening |
| `flourishing` | Which activities, relationships, capacities, achievements, or goods constitute doing or living well under which account across time? | rich account of living or functioning well |

## Root factorization

```text
prudential-evaluation-use
  := object or state, subject or beneficiary, purpose, and perspective
   x evaluative account, value bearer, respect, standard, and reasons
   x baseline, change, contribution, benefit, burden, and distribution
   x welfare subject, scope, time, components, aggregation, and evidence
   x harm event or deprivation, counterfactual or temporal basis, severity, and duration
   x flourishing activities, relationships, capacities, achievements, and opportunity
   x preference, experience, objective goods, virtue, rights, and competing accounts
   x uncertainty, tradeoffs, authority, limitations, and provenance
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Good vs. good for | either is favorable evaluation | value under an account vs. subject-relative prudential value |
| Benefit vs. value | benefits are valued positively | improvement for subject/purpose vs. general evaluation or importance |
| Welfare vs. happiness | happiness may contribute to welfare | how well life is going vs. psychological state or satisfaction sense |
| Prudential harm vs. hazard consequence | hazards can cause harm | worsening under welfare account vs. adverse event/damage pathway |
| Harm vs. wrong | wrongdoing may harm | prudential worsening vs. violation of a norm, duty, or right |
| Welfare vs. flourishing | flourishing can be a welfare theory | general faring-well account vs. richer capacity/activity/relationship account |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Cost/value/utility, hazard/exposure/harm, emotion, health, justice, and deontic
owners retain their scopes. This Table stops before measuring population
welfare, making clinical quality-of-life judgments, choosing a universal theory
of the good, maximizing welfare, or recommending policy or individual action.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify the value bearer, subject or beneficiary, purpose, respect, scope, and time.
2. State the evaluative or welfare account before comparing outcomes.
3. Select general good, benefit, welfare state, prudential harm, or flourishing account.
4. Record baseline, counterfactual or temporal basis, components, distribution, and evidence.
5. Keep preference, experience, objective goods, virtue, rights, and capabilities available as competing accounts.
6. Preserve burdens, tradeoffs, disagreement, uncertainty, authority, and limitations.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Well-being theory | supplies competing accounts of what is good for a subject | requires account, subject, scope, components, and time |
| Evaluation system | scores benefits, burdens, or outcomes | keeps source states and normative aggregation separate |
| Risk or safety analysis | traces adverse events and consequences | separates hazard pathways from welfare-relative harm |

## Failure signs

- good, useful, beneficial, valuable, and preferred are treated as synonyms;
- welfare is reduced automatically to income, preference, happiness, or health;
- any damage event is called prudential harm without a subject and baseline;
- benefit to one subject is treated as benefit to all;
- flourishing is asserted without an account or opportunity conditions;
- a candidate Table supplies policy, clinical, or personal advice.
