# Public Policy, Program, Service, Regulation, and Implementation

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Public policy states an authorized public direction, objective, or course. A public program organizes resources and activities to pursue specified public objectives. A public service provides a governed benefit, function, or access to recipients or the public. Regulation uses public instruments to shape conduct or conditions. Implementation translates an authorized policy, law, or program design into operational action and delivery.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `public-policy` | Which public authority adopts which direction, objective, or course for which problem and population? | authorized public direction |
| `public-program` | Which governed arrangement uses which resources and activities to pursue which public objectives? | organized public intervention |
| `public-service` | Which provider delivers which governed function, benefit, or access to which recipients under what conditions? | public-facing provision |
| `regulation` | Which public instrument shapes which conduct or conditions for what objective under which authority? | public conduct-shaping instrument |
| `policy-implementation` | Which actors translate which authorized design into which operational actions, outputs, and delivery? | policy-to-operation process |

## Root factorization

```text
public-action-use
  := public problem, affected population, jurisdiction, authority, and time
   x policy objective, adopted direction, instrument mix, assumptions, and tradeoffs
   x program governance, eligibility, resources, activities, outputs, and evaluation
   x service provider, recipient, access, quality, continuity, and accountability
   x regulatory subject, instrument, requirement, incentive, monitoring, and enforcement
   x implementers, capacity, coordination, procedure, adaptation, and delivery
   x adoption, implementation fidelity, reach, output, outcome, and unintended effect
   x evidence, equity, uncertainty, version, and provenance
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Policy vs. law | law may authorize policy | public direction/course vs. legal status and source |
| Policy vs. program | programs enact policies | governing direction vs. organized resources and activities |
| Program vs. service | programs may deliver services | intervention arrangement vs. recipient-facing provision |
| Regulation vs. rule | regulation may contain rules | public instrument/system vs. individual conditional standard |
| Adoption vs. implementation | either is a policy stage | authorized decision vs. operational translation |
| Output vs. outcome | outputs may contribute to outcomes | delivered product/activity vs. wider resulting change |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Generic policy/rule, organization, governance/compliance, choice, evaluation, and outcome retain their owners. This Table stops before choosing policy, determining eligibility, assessing regulatory compliance, allocating services, or recommending public action.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify public problem, population, jurisdiction, authority, objective, and time.
2. Select policy direction, program arrangement, service provision, regulation, or implementation process.
3. Record authorization, resources, actors, instruments, delivery, monitoring, and review separately.
4. Separate adoption, implementation, output, reach, outcome, and impact claims.
5. Preserve affected perspectives, distribution, adaptation, failure, uncertainty, and provenance.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Public governance framework | maps policy cycle and institutional capacities | separates stages without assuming a linear universal cycle |
| Program logic | relates inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes | keeps authorization and service relationship explicit |
| Regulatory system | specifies instruments, compliance, and enforcement | separates regulation from an individual rule |

## Failure signs

- policy adoption proves implementation or outcome;
- law, policy, program, service, and regulation are interchangeable;
- recipients, implementers, authority, or jurisdiction are unnamed;
- output counts are presented as social outcomes;
- one idealized policy cycle is universalized;
- the Table recommends policy or decides eligibility/compliance.
