# Translation, Paraphrase, Equivalence, Adaptation, and Transcreation

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Translation produces an expression in a target language from a source expression under a declared purpose. Paraphrase produces a differently worded expression, usually within one language, intended to preserve selected content or function. Equivalence is a claimed relation of sameness or adequacy along stated dimensions and thresholds. Adaptation transforms an expression or work for a new medium, audience, setting, purpose, or constraint. Transcreation is a purpose-driven creative transformation, often across languages and cultures, prioritizing selected effects or functions over close formal correspondence.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `translation` | Which source expression is rendered in which target language for which purpose and audience? | cross-language rendering |
| `paraphrase` | Which expression is reformulated while preserving which selected content or function? | reformulation |
| `equivalence` | Which two items are equivalent in which respects, under which criteria and threshold? | declared relation claim |
| `adaptation` | Which source is transformed for which medium, audience, context, purpose, or constraint? | context-changing transformation |
| `transcreation` | Which intended effects or functions guide which creative cross-language/cultural transformation? | effect-prioritized transformation |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Translation vs. paraphrase | both reformulate expressions | target-language change vs. normally same-language rewording |
| Translation vs. equivalence | translation may claim equivalence | transformation activity/product vs. dimension-specific relation claim |
| Translation vs. adaptation | both derive from a source | language-centered rendering vs. broader audience/medium/context transformation |
| Adaptation vs. transcreation | both permit creative change | general contextual transformation vs. explicit effect/function priority across language/culture |
| Formal vs. functional equivalence | both compare source and target | correspondence of form vs. adequacy of use/effect |
| Work vs. translated expression | translations realize a work under some models | abstract work identity vs. language-specific realization |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Meaning/Reference owns semantic interpretation; Similarity/Equivalence owns general match logic; Abstract Work owns work/expression ontology; Register/Style owns linguistic manner and audience variety. This Table stops before certifying fidelity, legal authorization, cultural appropriateness, or equivalence without declared criteria and qualified review.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify source and target, language/modality, versions, purpose, audience, context, and stakes.
2. Select translation, paraphrase, equivalence assessment, adaptation, or transcreation.
3. Declare what is intended to be preserved and what may change.
4. Record responsible roles, review method, criteria, alternatives, losses, additions, and uncertainty.
5. Route meaning analysis, work identity, general similarity, and legal rights to their owners or qualified authorities.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| bilingual translation | changes language of an expression | requires purpose, audience, source/target versions, and review status |
| cataloging model | distinguishes work from translated expression | prevents language realization from silently replacing work identity |
| localization/creative brief | states target audience and intended effect | exposes permissible transformations and evaluation dimensions |

## Failure signs

- translation is assumed to guarantee complete semantic or pragmatic equivalence;
- paraphrase is treated as lossless identity;
- adaptation and transcreation are used without a declared purpose or change scope;
- source/target language, version, audience, or evaluator is omitted;
- creative contribution is confused with legal authorship or authorization;
- cultural references and material additions or omissions are hidden.
