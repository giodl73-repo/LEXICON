# Aesthetic Property, Experience, Judgment, Taste, and Value

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

An aesthetic property is a feature ascribed in aesthetic description or judgment under a declared account. An aesthetic experience is an encounter attended to as aesthetically salient, with perceptual, imaginative, affective, cognitive, embodied, or contextual dimensions. An aesthetic judgment attributes or evaluates aesthetic character under stated criteria and perspective. Taste is a person's or community's patterned disposition and cultivated capacity for aesthetic response or judgment under an account. Aesthetic value is the worth ascribed to an object, event, practice, or experience in aesthetic respects, not a synonym for liking, price, art status, or moral value.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `aesthetic-property` | Which aesthetic feature is ascribed to what, under which account and evidence? | aesthetically described feature |
| `aesthetic-experience` | Who encounters what as aesthetically salient, with which experiential dimensions and context? | aesthetic encounter |
| `aesthetic-judgment` | Who attributes or evaluates which aesthetic character under which criteria and reasons? | aesthetic appraisal act/result |
| `taste` | Which patterned disposition or cultivated capacity shapes whose aesthetic responses and judgments? | aesthetic response/judgment disposition |
| `aesthetic-value` | What worth is ascribed in which aesthetic respects, by whom, under which account and comparison? | aesthetic worth claim |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Aesthetic property vs. physical property | physical features may ground aesthetic ones | aesthetic-ascription role vs. measurement-independent physical description |
| Experience vs. judgment | judgments may arise from experiences | encounter/state vs. attributed or evaluative commitment |
| Judgment vs. taste | taste informs judgments | particular appraisal vs. patterned disposition/capacity |
| Liking vs. aesthetic value | liking can support value claims | response preference vs. worth under stated aesthetic reasons |
| Aesthetic vs. artistic value | artworks can have both | value in aesthetic respects vs. value as art, including non-aesthetic dimensions |
| Aesthetic judgment vs. fact report | judgments can cite facts | aesthetic attribution/evaluation vs. descriptive report alone |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Evaluation/Measure owns general criteria and scales; Appraisal/Feeling owns emotion episodes; Perception owns perceptual appearance; Good/Welfare owns prudential value; Artwork owns art status and descriptive form. This Table stops before claiming universal taste, converting disagreement into defect, inferring value from price or popularity, or treating one philosophical theory as settled.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify bearer, work/nonwork status, experiencer/evaluator, context, cultural/historical standpoint, and purpose.
2. Select property, experience, judgment, taste, or value question.
3. Declare vocabulary, criteria, comparison class, reasons, evidence, theory/account, and uncertainty.
4. Separate description, response, judgment, disposition, and value claim.
5. Preserve disagreement and alternative accounts; route empirical perception, emotion, general measurement, and prudential value to their owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| critical judgment | attributes and evaluates aesthetic character | requires criteria, reasons, perspective, comparison, and confidence |
| audience study | records responses or preferences | does not by itself establish aesthetic properties or value |
| philosophical account | relates experience, judgment, taste, and value | keeps account-relative dependencies and contrary positions visible |

## Failure signs

- beauty, pleasure, liking, aesthetic experience, judgment, and value are synonyms;
- an individual preference is universalized;
- popularity, price, prestige, or art status proves aesthetic value;
- descriptive physical properties automatically entail aesthetic properties;
- disagreement is erased rather than explained by criteria, perspective, or account;
- cultural and historical formation of taste is omitted.
