# Function, Impairment, Activity Limitation, Disability, and Participation

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Functioning encompasses body functions and structures, activities, and participation in interaction with context. An impairment is a problem or difference in body function or structure under stated criteria. An activity limitation is difficulty executing a task or action. Disability is an umbrella for negative aspects of interaction among a person with a health condition and contextual factors. Participation is involvement in a life situation; a participation restriction is a problem in that involvement.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `health-functioning` | How does which person function across body, activity, and participation domains in which context? | contextual functioning profile |
| `impairment` | Which body function or structure differs or presents a problem under which criteria and assessment? | body-level functioning difference/problem |
| `activity-limitation` | What difficulty does a person have executing which task under which capacity or performance conditions? | task-execution difficulty |
| `disability` | Which negative interaction among health condition, person, activity/participation, and context is described under which account? | contextual disability umbrella |
| `participation` | How and to what extent is a person involved in which life situation, with what facilitators or barriers? | life-situation involvement |

## Root factorization

```text
functioning-disability-use
  := person, health condition if relevant, life domain, setting, and time
   x body function, body structure, difference or problem, criteria, and severity qualifier
   x task or action, capacity, actual performance, assistance, and environment
   x life situation, involvement, choice, access, role, and participation qualifier
   x environmental facilitators and barriers, personal factors, supports, and accommodations
   x disability account, self-identification, classifier, purpose, and language preference
   x assessment instrument, reporter, version, evidence, uncertainty, and change
   x rights context, limitations, provenance, and no-diagnosis boundary
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Function vs. functioning | functions are components | particular body/role sense vs. integrated contextual profile |
| Impairment vs. disease | disease can cause impairment | body function/structure difference vs. pathological process/classification |
| Activity limitation vs. incapacity | either concerns difficulty | graded contextual execution difficulty vs. categorical inability claim |
| Disability vs. impairment | impairment may contribute | contextual umbrella/interaction vs. body-level component |
| Capacity vs. performance | either concerns task execution | ability in standardized/hypothetical conditions vs. actual context |
| Participation vs. attendance | attendance may evidence participation | involvement in life situation vs. presence alone |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Health/Disease, designed function, action/capacity, access, accommodation, identity, and measurement retain their owners. This Table stops before classifying a person, determining eligibility, evaluating work/school fitness, prescribing rehabilitation, or inferring functioning from diagnosis.

## Selection procedure

1. Center the person's stated perspective and language; identify domain, context, and purpose.
2. Select functioning profile, impairment, activity limitation, disability interaction, or participation.
3. Separate capacity from performance and body, task, and life-situation levels.
4. Record facilitators, barriers, supports, assistance, choice, and assessment source.
5. Preserve variation, change, self-identification, uncertainty, and qualified authority.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| WHO ICF | supplies body/activity/participation/context framework | retains qualifiers and interaction rather than diagnosis inference |
| Disability studies | supplies social, relational, identity, and rights critiques | prevents one clinical classification from becoming universal ontology |
| Assessment instrument | measures selected functioning domains | separates score, construct, and lived context |

## Failure signs

- diagnosis determines functioning or disability;
- impairment and disability are synonyms;
- actual performance is treated as context-free capacity;
- participation means physical presence;
- barriers and facilitators are omitted;
- the Table determines eligibility, fitness, or needed care.
