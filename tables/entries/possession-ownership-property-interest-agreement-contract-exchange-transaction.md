# Possession, Ownership, Property Interest, Agreement, Contract, Exchange, and Transaction

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Possession concerns custody or control; ownership is an authority-recognized
bundle or status concerning an object or interest; a property interest is a
particular right, claim, power, immunity, or encumbrance recognized under a
declared system. Agreement records aligned expressions or commitments; contract
is an agreement given specified legal or institutional effect; exchange is a
reciprocal transfer; transaction is a bounded recorded change among parties,
assets, obligations, or statuses.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `possession` | Who has which custody or control over what, when, and by which evidence? | custody or control relation |
| `ownership` | Which authority recognizes which subject as owner of what under which account and time? | authority-recognized status |
| `property-interest` | Which scoped right, claim, power, immunity, security, or encumbrance concerns which subject matter? | governed interest |
| `agreement` | Which parties express what aligned terms or commitments, with what assent and record? | coordinated commitment record |
| `contract` | Which agreement has which effect under which governing framework, jurisdiction, validity conditions, and version? | institutionally effective agreement |
| `exchange` | Which parties reciprocally transfer which items, rights, performances, or risks under what terms? | reciprocal transfer arrangement |
| `transaction` | Which bounded event changes which assets, obligations, custody, or statuses and how is it recorded? | state-changing recorded event |

## Root factorization

```text
property-transaction-use
  := parties, roles, capacity, identity, and representation
   x subject matter, asset, service, right, obligation, and status
   x custody, control, title or ownership account, and property interests
   x offer, assent, terms, consideration or exchange basis, and conditions
   x governing framework, jurisdiction, authority, version, and priority
   x transfer, performance, delivery, acceptance, and settlement
   x validity, breach, remedy, termination, revocation, and dispute
   x event record, provenance, time, valuation, and uncertainty
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Possession vs. ownership | either concerns a subject and thing | custody/control fact vs. authority-recognized status |
| Ownership vs. property interest | interests can compose ownership | broader status/account vs. one scoped governed incident |
| Agreement vs. contract | contracts involve agreement | aligned commitments generally vs. framework-recognized effects |
| Exchange vs. transaction | an exchange may be transacted | reciprocal transfer arrangement vs. bounded recorded state change |
| Transfer vs. permission | either changes what action is possible | change in custody/right/status vs. allow relation under policy |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Hohfeldian incidents, consent, authority, access permission, price/value,
inheritance, privacy, and software transactions retain their owners. Every
property or contract claim names its governing framework and jurisdiction.
This Table does not determine title, validity, remedy, tax, compliance, or
professional advice and does not enumerate property or contract types.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify parties, subject matter, time, framework, jurisdiction, and record.
2. Separate custody/control, ownership status, and particular interests.
3. Separate expressed agreement from framework-recognized contract effect.
4. Record reciprocal transfer separately from the transaction event and settlement.
5. Preserve conditions, third-party interests, disputes, invalidity, and uncertainty.
6. Route legal or accounting determinations to competent authorities.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Property system | defines recognized interests and transfer rules | requires authority, jurisdiction, time, and subject matter |
| Contract law | supplies formation, content, performance, and remedy rules | keeps agreement distinct from legal effect |
| Transaction system | records state changes and settlement | separates event record from underlying rights and obligations |

## Failure signs

- physical custody is treated as proof of ownership;
- ownership is represented as one universal indivisible right;
- agreement automatically implies an enforceable contract;
- exchange, sale, transfer, transaction, and settlement share one event;
- a software transaction is assumed to have legal effect;
- the Table supplies legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice.
