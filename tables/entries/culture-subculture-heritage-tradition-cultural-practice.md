# Culture, Subculture, Heritage, Tradition, and Cultural Practice

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Culture is a changing complex of meanings, practices, knowledge, values, forms, and material conditions associated with a society or group under a declared account. A subculture is a situated cultural formation differentiated within or across broader formations, not necessarily subordinate or homogeneous. Heritage is what communities, groups, institutions, or legal regimes recognize, transmit, interpret, and safeguard from the past for present and future significance. Tradition is a pattern represented and transmitted as continuous with a past while remaining open to recreation. A cultural practice is a recurrent or organized way of doing, making, expressing, or participating that carries cultural meaning in context.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `culture` | Which group's meanings, practices, forms, values, and conditions are described under which account? | cultural formation/account |
| `subculture` | Which formation is differentiated how, by whom, and relative to which broader contexts? | situated differentiated formation |
| `heritage` | What is recognized as inherited and significant by whom, under which process and authority? | recognized transmitted inheritance |
| `tradition` | Which pattern is represented and transmitted as continuous with which past, with what recreation? | past-oriented transmission pattern |
| `cultural-practice` | Which culturally meaningful activity is performed, maintained, changed, or contested by whom? | meaning-bearing practice |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Culture vs. society | societies carry cultures | meaning/practice formation vs. organized social collectivity |
| Culture vs. subculture | both name cultural formations | broader or differently scoped formation vs. relation of differentiation in context |
| Heritage vs. history | heritage refers to pasts | present recognition/transmission/significance vs. inquiry or account of past events |
| Tradition vs. unchanged repetition | traditions invoke continuity | transmitted and recreated pattern vs. literal invariance |
| Cultural practice vs. custom | both can recur | culturally interpreted activity vs. established habitual convention |
| Community recognition vs. external designation | institutions may designate heritage | participant recognition/meaning vs. outside classificatory act |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Person/Community/Society owns social grouping; Norm/Convention/Custom/Practice/Tradition owns generic normative recurrence; Institution owns status functions; Ritual owns ceremonial and sacred organization. This Table stops before treating cultures as bounded essences, assigning identity to persons, overriding community recognition, disclosing restricted knowledge, or making legal heritage determinations.

## Selection procedure

1. Declare people/groups, self-identification, place, time, standpoint, purpose, and source relationships.
2. Choose formation, differentiated formation, recognized inheritance, past-oriented transmission, or meaningful-practice question.
3. Record internal diversity, overlap, change, power, access, recognition, authority, and contestation.
4. Separate participant accounts, scholarly analysis, institutional designation, and legal status.
5. Preserve restricted or sacred access and route general group, norm, institution, and ritual questions to their owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| cultural description | interprets meanings and practices | requires standpoint, scope, internal variation, and evidence |
| heritage inventory | records recognized elements and safeguarding | distinguishes community recognition from institutional listing |
| transmission account | traces continuity and change | records recreation, interruption, revival, and contested history |

## Failure signs

- a culture or subculture is treated as homogeneous, timeless, or naturally bounded;
- heritage is assigned without the relevant community's recognition or participation;
- tradition means unchanged survival from an authenticated origin;
- an institutional list is treated as exhaustive cultural authority;
- observer interpretation is presented as participant self-understanding;
- restricted knowledge or access conditions are ignored.
