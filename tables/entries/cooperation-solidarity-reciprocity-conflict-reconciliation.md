# Cooperation, Solidarity, Reciprocity, Conflict, and Reconciliation

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Cooperation is coordinated contribution toward compatible or shared ends.
Solidarity is a relation or practice of identification, commitment, and support
among or with others, often under adversity. Reciprocity links a contribution,
benefit, burden, or response to another across a relationship or sequence.
Conflict is an incompatibility or struggle among aims, claims, actions,
identities, or arrangements. Reconciliation is a process or achieved condition
of repairing a damaged relationship after conflict or wrongdoing under a
declared account.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `cooperation` | Which participants coordinate which contributions toward what compatible or shared ends under which arrangement? | coordinated joint contribution |
| `solidarity` | Who identifies or stands with whom through which commitments, support, shared fate, or collective action under what adversity? | identification-and-support relation |
| `reciprocity` | Which contribution, benefit, burden, or response is linked to which prior or expected act across what relationship and time? | relational return principle |
| `social-conflict` | Which aims, claims, actions, identities, or arrangements are incompatible or contested by which parties and through what process? | contested social incompatibility |
| `reconciliation` | Which damaged relationship is repaired in what respects through which acknowledgment, truth, accountability, restitution, trust, or institutional change? | relationship-repair process or state |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Cooperation vs. coordination | cooperation requires coordination | compatible/shared contribution vs. ordering activity regardless of ends |
| Cooperation vs. solidarity | either can join participants | contribution toward ends vs. identification and commitment under a relation/adversity |
| Reciprocity vs. exchange | exchanges may be reciprocal | return principle across relationship/time vs. arranged reciprocal transfer |
| Conflict vs. competition | competition can create conflict | contested incompatibility/struggle vs. rivalry under a shared opportunity structure |
| Reconciliation vs. conflict cessation | either may reduce struggle | relational repair in named respects vs. absence or suspension of overt conflict |
| Reconciliation vs. forgiveness | forgiveness may contribute to repair | multi-dimensional relationship process vs. changed response by a wronged party |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Collective action shortfalls, incentives, market competition, coordinated work,
trust, agreement/exchange, justice, and future political/legal owners retain
their scopes. This Table stops before conflict-resolution advice, peace
negotiation, restorative-justice procedure, determining forgiveness, or
claiming that solidarity or reconciliation is always morally good.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify parties, relationship, aims or claims, history, power, boundary, and time.
2. Select coordinated contribution, solidarity relation, reciprocal return, conflict, or reconciliation question.
3. Record ends, commitments, expectations, incompatibilities, affected parties, and conduct separately.
4. For solidarity, test inclusion, outsider effects, coercion, and whether support is among or with others.
5. For reconciliation, name the damaged relationship and exact repair dimensions without assuming forgiveness or justice.
6. Preserve disagreement, noncooperation, power asymmetry, failed repair, uncertainty, and provenance.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Collective-action analysis | studies incentives, contribution, and provision | separates cooperation relation from outcome and shortfall |
| Solidarity theory | analyzes identification, commitment, support, and collective action | keeps among/with, outsiders, ends, and disvalue visible |
| Reconciliation or transitional-justice framework | supplies repair processes and institutional mechanisms | separates relationship dimensions from peace, justice, truth, and forgiveness claims |

## Failure signs

- coordination is assumed to mean shared purpose or moral cooperation;
- solidarity is any sympathy, donation, agreement, or group membership;
- reciprocity requires immediate equal exchange;
- competition and conflict are treated as identical;
- ending violence or dispute is automatically called reconciliation;
- reconciliation erases truth, accountability, restitution, disagreement, or affected-party agency;
- the Table prescribes a live conflict or justice process.
