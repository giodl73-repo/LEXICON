# State, Government, Polity, Administration, and Public Authority

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

A political state is a persisting institutional order claiming public authority over a population and territory. Government is the offices and officeholders that direct it for a period. A polity is the organized political community or constitutional order. Public administration carries authorized public functions into operation. A public authority is an institution or office empowered to make or execute public decisions within a jurisdiction.

| Sense | Governing question | Role |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| State vs. government | government directs state institutions | persisting order vs. current governing arrangement |
| State vs. society | populations overlap | public institutional order vs. wider social formation |
| Polity vs. population | either names a people | organized political order vs. counted inhabitants |
| Government vs. administration | both exercise public functions | political direction vs. operational execution |
| Public authority vs. official | officials may exercise authority | empowered institution/office vs. person occupying a role |
| Authority vs. capacity | either affects outcomes | authorized standing vs. practical ability |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Organization, institution, society, population, sovereignty, political power, and law retain their owners. This Table stops before recognizing a state, interpreting a constitution, deciding territorial claims, ranking regimes, or recommending institutional design.

## Selection procedure

1. State jurisdiction, territory, population, constitutional frame, and effective time.
2. Select persisting state, current government, polity, administration, or empowered public authority.
3. Record offices, competence, selection, term, functions, resources, and oversight separately.
4. Separate claimed authority, legal competence, practical capacity, legitimacy, and acceptance.
5. Preserve contested recognition, overlapping jurisdiction, institutional change, and uncertainty.

## Failure signs

- state, government, nation, and society are synonyms;
- current officeholders are treated as the persisting state;
- public authority is inferred from capacity or force alone;
- legality, legitimacy, justice, effectiveness, and popularity collapse;
- the Table resolves recognition, sovereignty, or constitutional disputes.
