# Communication, Conversation, Discourse, Narrative, and Dialogue

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Communication is a situated process in which participants produce, transmit,
receive, interpret, or jointly construct meaning through signs and channels.
Conversation is coordinated exchange among participants; discourse is an
extended organized use of language or signs in context; narrative organizes
events or states into a tellable sequence; dialogue is reciprocal engagement
oriented toward response, inquiry, understanding, or coordinated action.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `communication` | Who communicates what to or with whom, through which signs, channel, context, and feedback? | meaning-bearing interaction process |
| `conversation` | How do participants coordinate turns, topics, repair, and common ground across an exchange? | coordinated exchange |
| `discourse` | Which extended body of expression is organized by which context, practice, genre, or institution? | contextual communicative organization |
| `narrative` | Which events, agents, perspectives, and temporal or causal links are selected and ordered into an account? | event-organizing account |
| `dialogue` | Which reciprocal contributions pursue what inquiry, understanding, decision, or relationship? | responsive engagement form |

## Root factorization

```text
communicative-organization-use
  := participants, roles, audience, and relationship
   x content, referents, signs, language, and presuppositions
   x medium, channel, setting, timing, and accessibility
   x turns, sequence, topic, response, feedback, and repair
   x context, practice, institution, genre, and power conditions
   x events, temporal order, causal attribution, narrator, and perspective
   x purpose, uptake, misunderstanding, contestation, and outcome
   x record, provenance, privacy, and interpretation limits
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Communication vs. information transfer | either can move signals | situated meaning construction and uptake vs. transport of encoded content |
| Conversation vs. dialogue | either is reciprocal | interactional exchange generally vs. engagement framed by an inquiry or relational purpose |
| Discourse vs. genre | genre can organize discourse | contextual body/practice of expression vs. conventional class of expression |
| Narrative vs. chronology | either orders events | selected account with perspective and relations vs. temporal ordering alone |
| Speech act vs. conversation | speech acts occur in conversation | action performed by an utterance vs. multi-turn coordination |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Meaning, language variety, genre, stance, speech acts, information, interaction
protocols, and privacy retain their owners. This Table does not prescribe
communication technique, infer intent from uptake, or enumerate media,
discourse communities, narrative genres, or dialogue methods.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify participants, roles, content, context, medium, and purpose.
2. Select process, exchange, extended organization, event account, or reciprocal engagement.
3. Record sequence, feedback, repair, common ground, and contested assumptions.
4. For narrative, separate event order from narrator order and causal attribution.
5. Record uptake and misunderstanding without assuming successful transfer.
6. Preserve privacy, accessibility, power, provenance, and interpretation limits.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Communication model | relates sender, message, channel, and receiver | adds co-construction, context, feedback, and repair |
| Discourse analysis | studies situated extended language use | provides a cross-domain routing boundary without one school |
| Narrative theory | analyzes event organization and perspective | separates account structure from event chronology |

## Failure signs

- message transmission is assumed to guarantee shared meaning;
- conversation is reduced to alternating messages;
- discourse, genre, register, and ideology share one field;
- narrative order is treated as event order or causal proof;
- dialogue is treated as automatic agreement;
- participant intent is inferred solely from audience interpretation.
