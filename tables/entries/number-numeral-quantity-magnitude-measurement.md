# Number, Numeral, Quantity, Magnitude, and Measurement

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

A number is a mathematical object under a declared number system or structural account. A numeral is an expression denoting a number in a notation. A quantity is a property whose instances can be compared quantitatively under a declared kind. Magnitude is the size or ordering aspect of a number, quantity, vector, or other object under a specified comparison. This Table uses measurement as the cross-level pivot among measurand, quantity, magnitude, value, unit, procedure, and result; the operational measurement sense remains with Claim and Evidence.

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Number vs. numeral | numeral denotes a number | mathematical object vs. expression/representation |
| Number vs. numerical value | values use numbers | abstract object vs. number in a quantity-value representation |
| Quantity vs. amount | ordinary language overlaps | quantitatively comparable property vs. context-specific extent/count |
| Quantity vs. magnitude | quantities have magnitudes under accounts | property kind/instance vs. specified size/order aspect |
| Measurement route vs. calculation | measurement can compute | cross-level measurand/value pivot vs. derivation from stipulated inputs alone |
| Measurement route vs. result | measurement produces results | routing among operation and quantity roles vs. attributed value and uncertainty |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Quantity Value/Unit/Dimension/Conversion, comparative quantity, cardinality/ordinality, mathematical relation solving, and measurement quality retain operational ownership. This Table stops before computing, converting, calibrating, selecting a measurement method, or asserting one ontology of number or quantity.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify whether the use concerns object, notation, property, size/order aspect, or obtaining process.
2. State number system, notation, quantity kind, comparison, measurand, and procedure as applicable.
3. Route numerical value/unit/conversion and measurement quality to their owners.
4. Separate representation changes from changes in the denoted number or measured quantity.
5. Preserve conventions, uncertainty, traceability, and competing foundations.

## Failure signs

- number, numeral, numerical value, and quantity are synonyms;
- changing notation changes the number;
- magnitude has no named comparison or operator;
- a computed value is automatically an empirical measurement;
- result and measurement process collapse;
- this router duplicates unit, conversion, or calibration procedures.
