# Creation, Authorship, Performance, Interpretation, and Reception

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Creation is the process by which a work, expression, event, or practice is brought about through generative activity. Authorship is attributed responsibility for originating or shaping a work or expression under a declared artistic, scholarly, social, institutional, or legal account. Performance is an embodied, enacted, sounded, or otherwise eventive realization or presentation by performers in a setting. Interpretation is the activity or result of making sense of, explaining, or realizing a work or expression from a standpoint. Reception is the encounter, uptake, response, circulation, and afterlife of a work or performance among audiences and communities.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `creation` | Through which generative activities, constraints, materials, and collaborations did what come about? | generative process |
| `authorship` | Who is attributed which originating or shaping responsibility under which account and evidence? | attributed originating responsibility |
| `art-performance` | Who realizes or presents which work/practice how, where, when, and for whom? | eventive realization/presentation |
| `art-interpretation` | Who makes or realizes which sense of what from which standpoint and evidence? | sense-making or realization |
| `reception` | Which audiences or communities encounter, respond to, circulate, or reframe what over time? | audience/community uptake |

## Root factorization

```text
art-production-reception-use
  := work/expression/practice, version, medium, place, time, institutions, and context
   x creators/contributors, generative acts, materials/tools, constraints, collaboration, and sequence
   x authorship account, attributed responsibility, evidence, credit, authority, dispute, and legal distinction
   x performance event, performers, roles, score/script/tradition relation, setting, audience, and variation
   x interpretation object, standpoint, method, evidence, alternatives, realization choices, and uncertainty
   x reception audience/community, access, circulation, response, appropriation, reframing, and change over time
   x source, provenance, rights/access, contested accounts, and uncertainty
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Creation vs. authorship | authors may create | generative activity vs. attributed originating responsibility/status |
| Author vs. contributor | both may shape a work | authorship under an account vs. any bounded contribution role |
| Work vs. performance | performances may realize works | persistent/abstract identity vs. particular eventive realization |
| Performance vs. presentation | both bring work to an audience | enacted realization vs. broader act of showing or delivering |
| Interpretation vs. intention | intentions may inform interpretations | sense-making/realization vs. agent's purposive state |
| Reception vs. evaluation | reception includes judgments | historical/social uptake vs. particular assessment |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Abstract Work/Performance Instance owns ontological identity and compliance; Coordinated Work owns collaboration; Responsibility owns answerability; Meaning/Interpretation owns general semantic interpretation; Communication/Discourse owns exchange; Contribution/Legacy questions remain with their existing cross-domain owners. This Table stops before resolving legal authorship or copyright, authenticating attribution, inferring intention from a work alone, or treating one reception as definitive.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify work/expression/practice, version, participants, institutions, place/time, medium, context, and purpose.
2. Select generative process, authorship attribution, performance realization, interpretation, or reception question.
3. Declare the governing artistic, social, institutional, scholarly, or legal account and its evidence.
4. Record collaboration, credit, performance variation, interpretive alternatives, audience diversity, and historical change.
5. Separate descriptive contribution from authorship, work from event, intention from interpretation, and response from value.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| production record | documents people, acts, tools, and sequence | separates generative contribution from authorship attribution |
| score/script/performance record | relates a work to an eventive realization | preserves performer choices, setting, version, and compliance account |
| review/reception history | records interpretation and audience response | identifies standpoint, audience, circulation, and change over time |

## Failure signs

- creator, author, contributor, performer, interpreter, and audience are interchangeable;
- legal authorship is inferred from creative contribution or credit alone;
- a performance event is collapsed into the abstract work;
- authorial intention is treated as the only possible interpretation;
- one critic, market result, or audience is treated as total reception;
- collaboration, institutional power, access, or historical change is omitted.
