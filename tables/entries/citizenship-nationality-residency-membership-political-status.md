# Citizenship, Nationality, Residency, Membership, and Political Status

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Citizenship is membership in a political community with status, rights, duties, and possible participation. Nationality is a legal bond between a person and a state under an applicable system. Residency records authorized or factual residence under stated criteria. Political membership is inclusion in a polity under a declared account. Political status is a governed position carrying specified incidents.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `citizenship` | Who is a citizen of which polity, by what basis, with which incidents and effective time? | civic-political membership status |
| `nationality` | Which legal bond connects which person and state under what law and recognition? | person-state legal bond |
| `residency` | Who resides where under which factual or authorized criteria, duration, and status? | place-relative residence status |
| `political-membership` | Who counts as a member of which polity for which participation, protection, or obligation? | polity inclusion relation |
| `political-status` | Which governed position carries which rights, duties, protections, disabilities, or procedures? | institutional political position |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship vs. nationality | systems may align them | political membership bundle vs. person-state legal bond |
| Citizenship vs. residency | residents may gain incidents | political status vs. living in a place under criteria |
| Residency vs. domicile | both locate a person | residence fact/status vs. legally significant permanent-home relation |
| Membership vs. belonging | either concerns inclusion | criterion-governed relation vs. experienced identification |
| Status vs. identity | status may shape identity | institutional position vs. self/social characterization |
| Status vs. incidents | status carries incidents | position vs. particular rights, duties, or restrictions |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Personhood, geographic place, legal rights, identity, migration, and documentation retain their owners. This Table stops before deciding anyone's nationality, immigration status, voting eligibility, entitlement, removal risk, or right to enter or remain.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify person, polity/state, jurisdiction, territory, source, and effective time.
2. Select citizenship, nationality, residence, membership, or other political status.
3. Record acquisition, recognition, duration, incidents, change, loss, and review separately.
4. Distinguish factual residence from authorized status and status from documentation.
5. Preserve multiple, disputed, pending, or absent status and cross-system variation.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Citizenship theory | separates status, agency, and identity | requires polity, incidents, and account |
| Nationality law | defines person-state legal bonds | requires jurisdiction, version, authority, and effective time |
| Administrative record | evidences a status or residence | separates evidence from the status itself |

## Failure signs

- citizenship, nationality, residence, and belonging are interchangeable;
- an address proves legal residence or political membership;
- documentation is treated as the status rather than evidence;
- status in one jurisdiction is generalized globally;
- the Table determines a live person's rights or immigration position.
