# Statement, Proposition, Premise, Conclusion, and Argument

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

A statement is an assertoric expression or act in a language and context. A proposition is the truth-evaluable content represented under a declared semantic account. A premise is a statement or proposition offered or assumed in support. A conclusion is what an inference or argument presents as following or supported. An argument is an organized set of premises and a conclusion connected by an asserted inferential relation.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `statement` | Which assertoric expression or act occurs in which language, context, and use? | assertoric expression/act |
| `proposition` | Which truth-evaluable content is represented under which identity and semantic account? | propositionally represented content |
| `premise` | Which statement or proposition is assumed or offered in support of what conclusion? | inferential input role |
| `conclusion` | Which content is presented as following or supported by which premises and inference? | inferential output role |
| `argument` | Which premises and conclusion are organized by which asserted inferential relation and purpose? | premise-conclusion organization |

## Root factorization

```text
argument-unit-use
  := expression or act, language, speaker or author, context, and time
   x represented content, semantic account, truth conditions, and identity criterion
   x premise set, explicit or implicit status, order, and assumption role
   x conclusion, target claim, scope, and qualification
   x inferential link, argument form, purpose, audience, and presentation
   x assertion, supposition, question, command, explanation, and persuasion distinction
   x ambiguity, reconstruction, suppressed material, alternatives, and challenge
   x provenance, uncertainty, and limitations
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Statement vs. sentence | a sentence may express a statement | contextual assertoric use vs. linguistic expression type |
| Statement vs. proposition | a statement may express a proposition | expression/act vs. represented content |
| Premise vs. evidence | evidence may be stated as a premise | inferential role vs. epistemic support artifact/status |
| Premise vs. assumption | either may be taken as given | argument input role vs. acceptance status for a purpose |
| Conclusion vs. result | either is an output | inferential role vs. evaluated or produced outcome |
| Argument vs. explanation | either links reasons and claims | support for conclusion vs. account of why/how an accepted phenomenon occurs |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Claim/Evidence, speech acts, linguistic syntax, inference kind, validity, and explanation retain their owners. This Table stops before determining truth, acceptability, persuasive quality, evidential weight, or validity of a submitted argument.

## Selection procedure

1. State language, context, expression/act, semantic account, and purpose.
2. Separate expression from content and inferential role from epistemic status.
3. Identify explicit and reconstructed premises, conclusion, and asserted link.
4. Preserve ambiguity, suppressed premises, qualifications, and alternative reconstructions.
5. Route inference kind, validity, evidence, and rhetoric to their owners.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Logic text | formalizes premises, conclusions, and consequence | keeps expression/content and reconstruction explicit |
| Argumentation theory | studies situated reasons and audiences | separates argument object from argumentation activity |
| Evidence system | records support for claims | keeps evidence status distinct from premise role |

## Failure signs

- sentence, statement, proposition, and claim are synonyms;
- any earlier sentence is treated as a premise;
- premise status proves truth or evidential adequacy;
- conclusion means any final sentence or produced result;
- explanation and argument are collapsed;
- the Table declares an argument valid or convincing.
