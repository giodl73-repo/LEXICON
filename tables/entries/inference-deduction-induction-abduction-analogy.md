# Inference, Deduction, Induction, Abduction, and Analogy

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Inference is a transition from represented inputs to a conclusion under a rule, policy, or reasoning practice. Deduction claims consequence that preserves truth under a stated logic. Induction extends from observed cases or data toward unobserved cases or generalization. Abduction selects or advances a hypothesis through explanatory considerations. Analogy transfers a candidate relation or conclusion through a structured source-to-target mapping.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `inference` | Which conclusion is drawn from which inputs under what rule, procedure, or reasoning practice? | input-to-conclusion transition |
| `deduction` | Which conclusion is claimed to follow necessarily under which formal consequence relation? | consequence-preserving inference |
| `induction` | Which unobserved or general conclusion is supported from which observations, sample, or pattern under what method? | ampliative generalizing inference |
| `abduction` | Which hypothesis is advanced because it would explain what, relative to which alternatives and explanatory criteria? | explanatory hypothesis inference |
| `analogical-inference` | Which source-target mapping supports which candidate transfer through what relevant similarities and differences? | structure-transfer inference |

## Root factorization

```text
inference-use
  := reasoner or system, input representations, conclusion, context, and time
   x inference rule or policy, formal or informal status, direction, and warrant
   x logic, language, consequence relation, premises, and derivation
   x population, sample, observations, generalization target, and uncertainty
   x phenomenon, candidate explanations, explanatory criteria, and alternatives
   x source, target, correspondence, invariant structure, differences, and transfer
   x monotonicity, defeasibility, background assumptions, counterexamples, and revision
   x evidence, provenance, limitations, and confidence
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Inference vs. implication | either links contents | reasoning transition vs. semantic/formal relation |
| Deduction vs. induction | both move from inputs to conclusion | necessary consequence claim vs. ampliative generalization |
| Induction vs. abduction | both are defeasible/ampliative | observed-to-unobserved support vs. explanatory selection |
| Abduction vs. deduction | either may use hypotheses | explanation-guided proposal/support vs. consequence under a logic |
| Analogy vs. similarity | analogy uses similarities | structured mapping and transfer vs. likeness alone |
| Inference vs. causal process | reasoning may be causally realized | normative/formal transition vs. mechanism producing an event |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Argument roles, evidence, statistical inference, causal inference, similarity/analogy mapping, and logical validity retain their owners. This Table stops before certifying warrant, selecting a best explanation, generalizing from data, or endorsing a conclusion.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify inputs, conclusion, reasoner/system, context, and claimed warrant.
2. Select generic inference, deduction, induction, abduction, or analogy.
3. State formal system or method, background assumptions, alternatives, and defeasibility.
4. For analogy, record source-target correspondences and relevant differences.
5. Preserve counterexamples, revision conditions, uncertainty, and evidential limits.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Formal logic | defines deduction under a consequence relation | keeps system and semantic/proof account explicit |
| Statistics/science | supplies inductive methods | routes actual estimation to statistical owners |
| Explanation/analogy theory | analyzes hypothesis choice and transfer | preserves alternatives and mapping losses |

## Failure signs

- any plausible conclusion is called deductive;
- induction and statistical estimation are interchangeable;
- the best available explanation is treated as certainly true;
- analogy is licensed by surface similarity alone;
- defeasible inferences are treated as monotonic;
- the Table endorses a real conclusion without domain evidence.
