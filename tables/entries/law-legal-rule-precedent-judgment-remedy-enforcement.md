# Law, Legal Rule, Precedent, Judgment, Remedy, and Enforcement

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

Law is a jurisdictional institutional order of recognized legal sources, norms, powers, procedures, and decisions. A legal rule is a source-recognized norm or decisional standard. Precedent is a prior decision treated as bearing on later decisions under a doctrine. A legal judgment is an authorized adjudicative disposition. A remedy is an authorized response addressing a legally cognizable claim or violation. Enforcement makes or seeks to make legal requirements and decisions effective.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `law` | Which institutional legal order recognizes which sources, officials, subjects, procedures, and norms? | jurisdictional legal order |
| `legal-rule` | Which source-recognized norm or decisional standard applies to whom under what conditions? | legal normative standard |
| `precedent` | Which prior decision has what binding or persuasive bearing under which doctrine and court relation? | prior-decision authority relation |
| `legal-judgment` | Which authorized adjudicator issues which disposition for which matter through what procedure? | adjudicative legal disposition |
| `legal-remedy` | Which authorized response addresses which recognized claim, injury, violation, or risk? | legally authorized response |
| `legal-enforcement` | Which actor uses which process or measure to secure compliance with or effect for which law or decision? | legal effectuation process |

## Root factorization

```text
legal-use
  := legal system, jurisdiction, subject matter, parties, and effective time
   x recognized source, hierarchy, enactment or decision, text, and version
   x rule subject, conditions, legal consequence, exceptions, and interpretation
   x prior decision, material facts, holding, court relation, and precedential weight
   x adjudicator, claim, procedure, evidence, reasons, disposition, and finality
   x remedy basis, beneficiary, obligor, form, scope, and availability
   x enforcement authority, trigger, process, measure, compliance, and review
   x legality, merit, justice, uncertainty, contestation, and provenance
```

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Law vs. morality | law may use moral terms | institutional legal status vs. moral status |
| Legal rule vs. policy | policy may guide legal action | source-recognized legal norm vs. governing intent/program direction |
| Precedent vs. example | both use prior cases | doctrinal bearing vs. illustrative similarity |
| Judgment vs. rule | judgments may articulate rules | case disposition vs. general standard |
| Remedy vs. punishment | punishment can be a legal response | claim/violation-addressing response vs. penal sanction |
| Enforcement vs. validity | valid law may be unenforced | effectuation process vs. legal status |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Policy/rule, deontic force, Hohfeldian incidents, evidence, justice, and responsibility retain their owners. This Table stops before interpreting governing law, predicting a case, establishing legal validity, selecting a remedy, or advising conduct.

## Selection procedure

1. State legal system, jurisdiction, source hierarchy, version, and effective time.
2. Select legal order, rule, precedent, judgment, remedy, or enforcement process.
3. Record authority, parties, procedure, conditions, reasons, disposition, and review separately.
4. Distinguish holding from dicta, binding from persuasive bearing, and validity from enforcement.
5. Preserve conflict of laws, contested interpretation, appeal, uncertainty, and qualified authority.

## Failure signs

- a morally desirable rule is assumed to be law;
- legal validity proves justice or legitimacy;
- any prior decision is binding precedent;
- judgment, remedy, sanction, and enforcement are synonyms;
- source hierarchy, jurisdiction, version, or effective date is missing;
- the Table interprets law or advises a live matter.
