# Person, Individual, Social Actor, Group, Community, and Society

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

An individual is one counted or distinguished entity under a declared account.
A person is a subject recognized through biological, psychological, social,
moral, or legal criteria. A social actor is a participant treated as able to
occupy roles or act in a social setting. A group joins or classifies multiple
members under a membership basis. A community adds sustained relations,
belonging, place, practice, or shared concern. A society is a large-scale,
persisting field of institutions, relations, populations, and practices.

## Sense table

| Sense | Governing question | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `individual` | Which one entity is distinguished from others under which counting or identity account? | separately identified entity |
| `person` | Which subject is recognized as a person by which biological, psychological, social, moral, or legal criteria? | person-recognized subject |
| `social-actor` | Which participant is treated as occupying which roles or performing which socially interpreted actions? | role-bearing social participant |
| `social-group` | Which members constitute which group under what membership, interaction, identity, or classification basis? | plural social formation |
| `community` | Which participants sustain which relations, belonging, place, practice, or shared concern across time? | sustained relational collective |
| `society` | Which population, institutions, relations, practices, boundaries, and histories compose the large-scale social order in view? | large-scale social formation |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Individual vs. person | a person may be counted as an individual | separately distinguished entity vs. subject under personhood criteria |
| Person vs. persona | either concerns presentation of a subject | recognized subject vs. adopted or attributed presentation |
| Social actor vs. role | actors occupy roles | participant vs. position-relative expectations or permissions |
| Group vs. collection | either has members | social membership/interaction/identity basis vs. view-relative aggregation |
| Group vs. community | communities include participants | membership formation vs. sustained belonging or relational practice |
| Community vs. society | either is a social formation | bounded relational collective vs. large-scale institutional and population order |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Identity, agency, persona, organization, institution, population, kinship,
collective action, and geographic place retain their owners. This Table stops
before demographic taxonomies, protected-class determinations, philosophical
or legal personhood rulings, named communities, national societies, and claims
that one individualist or holistic theory universally governs social entities.

## Selection procedure

1. State the entity account, purpose, perspective, boundary, and time.
2. Ask whether the question concerns one entity, person recognition, social participation, plural membership, sustained community, or society-scale order.
3. Record membership, role, interaction, belonging, institutional, and population criteria separately.
4. Name the recognition or classification authority and contested alternatives.
5. Do not transfer agency, responsibility, belief, or intention from a group to every member.
6. Preserve historical change, overlapping membership, exclusion, uncertainty, and provenance.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| Social ontology | analyzes constituents and construction of social entities | keeps entity level, criteria, perspective, and competing accounts explicit |
| Sociology | studies groups, communities, institutions, populations, and societies | separates analytic owner questions from named classifications and cases |
| Legal or administrative system | recognizes persons, memberships, and statuses | requires authority, jurisdiction, version, and effective time |

## Failure signs

- every individual is assumed to be a person under every account;
- persona, role, actor, and person are used interchangeably;
- any collection of people is called a community;
- group properties or responsibility are copied automatically to members;
- society is treated as one organization or one homogeneous population;
- one social ontology is presented as a universal classification.
