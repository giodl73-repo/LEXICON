# Artwork, Medium, Form, Composition, Style, and Genre

Status: candidate anchor entry

## Orientation

An artwork is an identified work recognized or proposed as art under a declared historical, cultural, institutional, functional, or theoretical account. A medium is the material, technical, sensory, symbolic, or performative means through which a work is made or realized. Form is the organization or configuration through which elements and relations constitute a work. Composition is the particular arrangement and relation of elements within a work or realization. Style is a recognizable manner or pattern of making or appearance attributed at a declared level. Genre is a historically and socially organized category of works or practices defined by conventions, expectations, functions, forms, or lineages.

| Sense | Governing question | Role |

## Decisive distinctions

| Pair | Shared feature | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Artwork vs. artifact | many artworks are artifacts | art-status work under an account vs. intentionally made material object |
| Artwork vs. performance instance | performances can be artworks or realizations | identified art work/status vs. particular event realizing a work |
| Medium vs. material | media use materials | organized means of realization vs. physical substance alone |
| Form vs. composition | composition instantiates organization | general/configurational organization vs. particular arrangement |
| Style vs. genre | both classify patterns | manner/features at an attributed level vs. conventional category and expectations |
| Style vs. register | both vary manner | artistic making/appearance vs. situational language variety |

## Dependencies and stopping boundary

Abstract Work/Performance Instance owns work ontology; Material Artifact owns objects and making; Register/Genre owns linguistic variation; Pattern/Motif owns general recurrence; Aesthetic Property/Value owns evaluation. This Table stops before declaring a universal definition of art, authenticating or attributing a work, valuing it, resolving ownership, or treating one art-historical taxonomy as timeless.

## Selection procedure

1. Identify work, realization/instance, maker attribution if relevant, place/time, community/institution, and purpose.
2. Select art status, medium, form, composition, style, or genre question.
3. State criteria, comparison set, historical/cultural account, source, and uncertainty.
4. Separate descriptive features from attribution, classification, interpretation, and evaluation.
5. Preserve hybrids, contested status, changing categories, and non-Western or practice-specific accounts.

## Reference Delta

| Reference form | Typical contribution | Lexicon delta |
|---|---|---|
| catalog record | identifies work, maker, date, medium, and dimensions | separates descriptive metadata from art-status, interpretation, and value claims |
| formal analysis | describes organization and composition | requires declared elements, relations, work/instance, and perspective |
| style/genre history | groups works by patterns and lineages | requires comparison set, period, community, criteria, and counterexamples |

## Failure signs

- artwork, artifact, physical object, abstract work, and performance event are collapsed;
- medium means only raw material;
- form, composition, style, and genre are synonyms;
- a style or genre attribution lacks a level, comparison set, history, or evidence;
- art status or taxonomy is presented as universal and uncontested;
- descriptive classification silently becomes aesthetic evaluation.
